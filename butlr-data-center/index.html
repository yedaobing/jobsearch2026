Data Center Program Workflow

The data center line runs like a seed-stage startup inside Butlr. Customers are smaller sites, such as containerized data centers, that start with a small POC and grow in steps. The thermal sensors replace manual FLIR scans done every 3 to 6 months by watching the power path continuously. This role owns the work end to end, from the first customer conversation to a full site or fleet.

Overview

Five phases and their owners

Each box shows the accountable owner below the line. The Data Center PM owns every phase today, with Sales at the start and the customer success team taking over operations once the vertical grows. The loops below the track show where work flows back, including the step that repeats for each expansion.

  1. L1A failed validation sends the kit back for rework: placement, thresholds, firmware or more units.
  2. L2Open punch-list items or a customer who won't sign off reopen validation.
  3. L3Each growth step (POC, then expansion, then full site or fleet) starts again at plan and prepare, using lessons from the last step.
  4. L4A new use case or form factor, such as in-rack sensing, goes back to scoping.
  5. L5Product gaps found in the field and in operations go to product and engineering with an owner, and feed the roadmap.

POC

About 6 racks and PDUs. Prove the sensors catch hotspots on the customer's power path. Kits ship from US stock.

Expansion

About 5 more sets at the same customer. DC-2 to DC-5 repeat with what the POC taught.

Full site or fleet

The whole data center or fleet. At-scale steps switch on: shipping from Shenzhen, customs, feeds into customer tools, handoff to customer success.

Operating rules

Rules that apply across phases

Risk priority

  1. Customer's live operations
  2. Alert accuracy
  3. Schedule

Install without disrupting a running site, and don't promise alerts the sensors can't back up.

Grow in steps

POC, then expansion, then full site or fleet. Each step must prove value before the next one is scoped.

At-scale steps wait

Customs, feeds into customer tools and the customer success handoff start when volume needs them, not before. Keep today's process light.

Every issue has an owner

No debugging without a named owner and a date. A named person validates each install rather than taking the installer's word for it.

Where the role can grow once the vertical is established: a repeatable POC playbook, reference designs by site type, and portfolio planning across many customers.
