Data Center Supply Chain Workflow

How hardware moves for a Butlr data center deployment, from the first demand signal to field returns and the expansion order: each stage, its major tasks, who owns them, and where Supply Chain and the TPM hand work to each other.

The stages map onto gates G1 to G5 of the data center program workflow. See also how Butlr runs its business.

Overview

Eight stages in four bands

Supply Chain leads while the kit is built and moved. The TPM leads at both ends: defining what the customer needs, and everything at the customer's site. Dashed markers show the program gates.

TPM owns: what and when

Demand, the kit for each site, the date it must be on site, site readiness, release to ship, receiving, install, and the customer conversation about dates.

Supply Chain owns: how it is built and moved

Suppliers, the contract manufacturer, build, test and QC, inventory, freight, export and import paperwork, customs, and failure analysis.

Engineering owns: design and certification

Approves the kit BOM, firmware versions and test specs; holds product certifications by country (FCC, CE, and others).

Across stages

Operating rules to agree with Supply Chain

Dates

  1. No delivery date reaches a customer until it is checked against lead times and stock (I2).
  2. Supply Chain commits only to signed orders; the forecast (I1) is for planning.

Shipping

  1. Nothing ships until it passes the ship gate (I7) and the site is ready (I8).
  2. The customer hears hardware status from one voice: the TPM.

Changes and stock

  1. Changes after lock get a cost and date impact and a decision within 48 hours (I6).
  2. Finish kits only against a firm order, so excess stock stays as parts, not finished kits.

Built from the Senior Program Manager, Data Operations job description, the hiring manager conversation on 5 Nov 2026 (small POCs shipped from Burlingame, no customs today), Butlr's data center, sensor and support policy pages, the Heatic 2+ FCC test report, and general hardware delivery practice. Butlr does not publish its supply chain organization, contract manufacturer or lead times, so owners and interfaces are a proposed operating model to confirm with the VP of Supply Chain.
