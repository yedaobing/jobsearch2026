Data Center Supply Chain Workflow

How hardware moves for a Butlr data center deployment, from the first demand signal to field returns and the expansion order: each stage, its major tasks, who owns them, and where Supply Chain and the TPM hand work to each other.

The stages map onto gates G1 to G5 of the data center program workflow. See also how Butlr runs its business.

Overview

Eight stages in four bands

Supply Chain leads while the kit is built and moved. The TPM leads at both ends: defining what the customer needs, and everything at the customer's site. Dashed markers show the program gates.

TPM owns: what and when

Demand, the kit for each site, the date it must be on site, site readiness, release to ship, receiving, install, and the customer conversation about dates.

Supply Chain owns: how it is built and moved

Suppliers, the contract manufacturer, build, test and QC, inventory, freight, export and import paperwork, customs, and failure analysis.

Engineering owns: design and certification

Approves the kit BOM, firmware versions and test specs; holds product certifications by country (FCC, CE, and others).

Plan
S0

Demand planning

Runs all the time, not once per deal.

  • Turn the pipeline into a forecast by deal stage, kit type and ship month (with Sales)
  • Check every customer date against lead times before it is given
I1 Demand forecast Weekly

Each opportunity by deal stage, the kit it implies, likely ship month. Planning only until signed.

I2 Lead times and stock Weekly · on change

Lead time and on-hand stock for each critical part; allocation warnings.

  • Keep lead times and stock current for the thermal sensor array, radio module and Hives
  • Decide pre-buys for long-lead parts (with Finance)
S1

Scope and define the kit

TPM leads.

  • Size the POC and design sensor placement per rack, PDU, busbar and cabinet (with hardware engineering)
  • Build the kit BOM, including spares; engineering approves
  • Flag international sites: certification, importer of record, power and LTE variants
I3 Kit BOM At G1

Sensor types and counts, Hives, PoE parts, mounts, cables and spares for each site.

  • Confirm a feasible ship date for the BOM
  • Check stock and long-lead exposure
G1 · POC scoped
Build and move · Supply Chain leads
S2

Order, build and test

Supply Chain executes; TPM tracks.

  • Release the order once the POC or expansion is signed
  • Track procurement against the required-on-site date; raise exceptions only
  • Run change decisions after the kit is locked
I4 Order release At signature

Signed scope, kit BOM, required-on-site date, ship-to site.

I5 Committed ship date Within days of I4

The date Supply Chain commits to, with any constraint.

I6 Change after lock Decided in 48 h

Requested change with its cost and date impact; TPM, Supply Chain and the engineering lead decide.

  • Place POs; build or pick the kit with the CM
  • Commit a ship date
  • Give cost and date impact for any change
S3

Ship gate and release

Shared: both sides sign off.

  • Confirm the site is ready: access, delivery window, gateway network and security approvals, storage
  • Release to ship
I7 Ship-gate results Before each shipment

Firmware version, Hive pairing, test record per unit, labels by rack or cabinet, spares, battery paperwork.

I8 Release to ship Before each shipment

Site ready, delivery window, contact on site, importer of record and local details.

  • Configure and test: firmware, Hive pairing, burn-in, labels; QC sign-off
  • Book freight; dangerous-goods paperwork for lithium batteries
S4

Freight, customs and receive

Supply Chain moves it; TPM receives it.

  • Get the customer's import and delivery inputs: importer of record, dock rules
  • Track the shipment; tell the customer about any delay
  • Receive on site: count against the BOM, check damage, record serial to location
I9 Shipment and customs status Until delivered

Tracking, customs holds, missing documents, revised arrival.

I10 Receiving report On arrival

Count against the BOM, damage, serial number to install location.

  • Export and import paperwork, HS codes, duties, broker
  • Clear customs and deliver to site

At the site · TPM leads
S5

Install and validate

TPM leads.

  • Line up install crews with hardware arrival
  • Swap units that fail on arrival from spares
  • Send failed units back tagged with lot, firmware and location
I11 Returns and failure analysis On each failure

Tagged units go to Supply Chain; root cause and fix come back.

  • Failure analysis and corrective action with the CM and quality
G3 · Validation passed
S6

Accept and hand over

TPM leads.

  • Hand over the as-built serial list: sensor to breaker, rack or PDU
  • Close the supply chain punch list
  • Return unused POC units and spares
I13 Unused stock return At G4 · project close

POC units and spares not installed, returned or redeployed so they don't become excess inventory.

  • Ship replacements still owed
  • Receive returned stock back into inventory

Operate and expand

Shared, ongoing.

  • Handle field failures and RMAs on the customer side
  • Forecast battery swaps from battery data and install dates; batch them by site
  • Turn the expansion into a new order
I11 Field returns On each failure

Same loop as S5 for the installed base.

I12 Battery and spares demand Monthly

Forecast swaps and spares by site and month.

Loops back to S2: about 5 more sets, then the full site or fleet.

Forecast against actual, slips and their causes, process changes.

  • Battery and spares replenishment

Across stages

Operating rules to agree with Supply Chain

Dates

  1. No delivery date reaches a customer until it is checked against lead times and stock (I2).
  2. Supply Chain commits only to signed orders; the forecast (I1) is for planning.

Shipping

  1. Nothing ships until it passes the ship gate (I7) and the site is ready (I8).
  2. The customer hears hardware status from one voice: the TPM.

Changes and stock

  1. Changes after lock get a cost and date impact and a decision within 48 hours (I6).
  2. Finish kits only against a firm order, so excess stock stays as parts, not finished kits.
