Data Center Supply Chain Workflow

How hardware moves for a Butlr data center deployment, from the first demand signal to field returns and the expansion order: each stage, its major tasks, who owns them, and where Supply Chain and the TPM hand work to each other.

The stages map onto gates G1 to G5 of the data center program workflow. See also how Butlr runs its business. For screen sharing: full-screen handoff diagram.

Overview

Eight stages in four bands

Supply Chain leads while the kit is built and moved. The TPM leads at both ends: defining what the customer needs, and everything at the customer's site. Dashed markers show the program gates.

TPM owns: what and when

Demand, the kit for each site, the date it must be on site, site readiness, release to ship, receiving, install, and the customer conversation about dates.

Supply Chain owns: how it is built and moved

Suppliers, the contract manufacturer, build, test and QC, inventory, freight, export and import paperwork, customs, and failure analysis.

Engineering owns: design and certification

Approves the kit BOM, firmware versions and test specs; holds product certifications by country (FCC, CE, and others).

Stage tasks

Major tasks in each stage, by owner

StageTPMSupply Chain
S0Demand planning
  • Turn the pipeline into a forecast by deal stage, kit type and ship month (with Sales)
  • Check every customer date against lead times before it is given
  • Keep lead times and stock current for the thermal sensor array, radio module and Hives
  • Decide pre-buys for long-lead parts (with Finance)
S1Scope and define the kit
  • Size the POC and design sensor placement (with hardware engineering)
  • Build the kit BOM, including spares
  • Flag international sites: certification, importer of record, power and LTE variants
  • Confirm a feasible ship date for the BOM
  • Check stock and long-lead exposure
S2Order, build and test
  • Release the order once the POC or expansion is signed
  • Track procurement against the required-on-site date; raise exceptions only
  • Run change decisions after the kit is locked
  • Place POs; build or pick the kit with the CM
  • Commit a ship date
  • Give cost and date impact for any change
S3Ship gate and release
  • Confirm the site is ready: access, delivery window, gateway network and security approvals, storage
  • Release to ship
  • Configure and test: firmware, Hive pairing, burn-in, labels; QC sign-off
  • Book freight; dangerous-goods paperwork for lithium batteries
S4Freight, customs and receive
  • Get the customer's import and delivery inputs: importer of record, dock rules
  • Track the shipment; tell the customer about any delay
  • Receive on site: count, damage, serial to location
  • Export and import paperwork, HS codes, duties, broker
  • Clear customs and deliver to site
S5Install and validate
  • Line up install crews with hardware arrival
  • Swap units that fail on arrival from spares; send them back tagged
  • Failure analysis and corrective action with the CM and quality
S6Accept and hand over
  • Hand over the as-built serial list: sensor to breaker, rack or PDU
  • Close the supply chain punch list
  • Return unused POC units and spares
  • Ship replacements still owed
  • Receive returned stock back into inventory
S7Operate and expand
  • Handle field failures and RMAs on the customer side
  • Forecast battery swaps; batch them by site
  • Turn the expansion into a new order
  • RMA returns, analysis and replacement stock
  • Battery and spares replenishment
  • Build the expansion order

Across stages

Operating rules to agree with Supply Chain

Dates

  1. No delivery date reaches a customer until it is checked against lead times and stock.
  2. Supply Chain commits only to signed orders; the demand forecast is for planning.

Shipping

  1. Nothing ships until it passes the ship gate and the TPM has released it to ship.
  2. The customer hears hardware status from one voice: the TPM.

Changes and stock

  1. Changes after lock get a cost and date impact and a decision within 48 hours.
  2. Finish kits only against a firm order, so excess stock stays as parts, not finished kits.

Built from the Senior Program Manager, Data Operations job description, the hiring manager conversation on 5 Nov 2026 (small POCs shipped from Burlingame, no customs today), Butlr's data center, sensor and support policy pages, the Heatic 2+ FCC test report, and general hardware delivery practice.
