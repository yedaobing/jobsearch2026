Hardware Deployment Workflow

The hardware TPM's scope starts when facilities hands over a data hall row and ends when that row is energized and handed to customer engineering. Delivery runs row by row, with seven gates between plan and handoff.

Overview

Seven milestones and their owners

Each box shows who does the work below the line. M0 runs once for the build; M1 to M6 repeat for every row. Gates G0 to G6 sit between milestones, and a row moves on only when its gate passes.

  1. NRAfter G6, the next row starts at M1 and M2. Rows overlap: while one row energizes, the next is being connected and the one after is receiving materials.
  2. FXA failed gate goes back to that milestone's owner to fix and re-test. A G1 failure goes back to the facility team and GC as a punch list.
  3. PLM1 and M2 run in parallel, and so do the three M4 connection streams. All three streams must pass before the G4 go / no-go.

Before this workflow

The TPM coordinates with the facility team, GC and other TPMs on construction and gray space fit-out. Facilities delivers white space power through the busway and cooling through the rack drop. Design and procurement run in parallel; the TPM tracks them as upstream inputs.

Row by row

Hardware is delivered one row at a time, from facility handover through rack energization, with a gate check at every milestone.

Handoff

Each energized row goes to customer engineering for further testing and platform engineering.

Upstream readiness

Design and procurement inputs

Design and procurement happen before or during construction, and the hardware TPM doesn't own them. Facilities, customer engineering, network engineering and supply chain do. The TPM tracks each input against row need-by dates, so a late item shows up as a risk weeks ahead instead of a failed gate on the floor.

Track, don't own

The TPM doesn't design or buy. The job is visibility: one list of inputs with owners, dates and status, reviewed weekly with partner EMs and TPMs.

Need-by dates

Each input gets a need-by date per row, planned back from the milestone that uses it. A projected miss becomes a RAID item with an owner before the row starts.

Design freeze

After freeze, every change goes through change control, so its impact on tap-offs, hoses, cutsheets and row dates is visible. Late rack configuration changes are a common cause of a failed G4.

Worth asking the hiring manager: how early does the role engage, from design freeze or only once racks ship?

TPM role

What the hardware TPM does

The TPM doesn't do the hands-on work. The job is to own the sequence, the gate checklists and the go / no-go, and to make sure every blocker has an owner.

MilestoneTPM actions
M0Own the RACI and demarcation. Stand up the upstream readiness tracker for design inputs and long-lead items. Build the integrated schedule with partner EMs and TPMs (supply chain, network, compute). Define gate criteria. Set the cadence and escalation path.
M1Schedule and lead the row walk-down. Drive the facility punch list to closure. Don't accept a row with blocking items.
M2Track inbound shipments against row dates. Reconcile against the BOM. Drive expedites and RMAs for shortages and damage. Flag at-risk rows early.
M3Confirm crew and rigging windows. Sequence racks so aisles aren't blocked. Check placement against the layout.
M4Run power, liquid and network connections in parallel. Share the coolant fill schedule with facilities so they can top up the loop. Route each failure to its owner with evidence. Keep the per-rack checklist current.
M5Chair the go / no-go. Publish the energization order and stagger. Be on site. Log results. Decide hold or de-energize with the technical leads.
M6Assemble the handoff package. Run the walk-through with customer engineering. Track open items to closure. Capture lessons for the next row.

Daily, on site

  1. Stand-up with crews: rows in each stage, today's targets, safety notes.
  2. Every blocker has one owner, one next action and a due date.
  3. Share tomorrow's fill and energization plan with facility operations.

Weekly

  1. Program health to leadership: rows by stage, gate pass rate, slips, top risks and mitigations.
  2. Dependency review with partner EMs and TPMs: supply, network, firmware, staffing.
  3. Look ahead at facility handovers and deliveries.
  4. Fold lessons learned into the gate checklists.

Hold the gate

No row is accepted and no rack is energized past an open gate. A one-day slip costs less than rework, or a leak, on an energized rack.
