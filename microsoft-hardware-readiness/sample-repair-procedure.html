Section 1

Prepare

Reusable module
  1. Confirm the asset tag, rack location and server model match the ticket.
  2. Confirm the replacement part number matches the ticket and the failed DIMM's slot.
  3. Confirm the node is out of production in the ticketing system.
Why it's written this way

Wrong server and wrong part are the two cheapest mistakes to prevent. Checking both before touching anything costs a minute.

Section 2

Power down and remove the server

Reusable module
  1. Power off the server. Confirm all power LEDs are off.
  2. Label and disconnect the rear cables.
  3. Put on an ESD wrist strap and connect it to the rack ground point.
  4. Position the server lift. Slide the server out until the rails lock, then release and lift it onto the ESD mat.
Why it's written this way

Labeling cables before removal prevents swapped ports on reinstall. The lift step is here because of a known rail issue; it is the kind of control added when hardware can't change.

Section 3

Open the chassis and remove the air baffle

Reusable module
  1. Loosen the [2] captive screws and slide the top cover back.
  2. Lift out the air baffle.
Why it's written this way

Sections 1 to 3 are shared by every repair on this platform. They are written and approved once, then assembled into each new procedure.

Section 4

Remove the GPU cage

Platform-specific
  1. Disconnect cables [C1–C4] from the GPU baseboard. Note their positions.
  2. Loosen the [4] cage screws in the order shown in Figure 4.
  3. With two hands on the cage handles, lift it straight up and place it on the ESD mat.
CAUTIONDo not touch the GPU contacts or the cold plate surfaces.
Why it's written this way

This section is why the procedure exists. On a standard server the generic FRU DIMM procedure works, but here the GPU cage is in the way, so the repair is new, unique, difficult or different (NUDD) and gets its own content.

The screw order keeps the cage from tilting onto the baseboard.

Section 5

Replace the DIMM

Repair-specific
  1. Locate slot [D7] using the slot map on the air baffle or in the service manual.
  2. Press both slot latches outward and lift the DIMM out.
  3. Align the notch on the new DIMM with the key in the slot. It fits only one way.
  4. Press down evenly until both latches click closed.
NOTEPlace the failed DIMM in an anti-static bag and attach the RMA label.
Why it's written this way

The only steps unique to this repair. Calling out the keyed notch heads off the most likely mistake: forcing a part in the wrong way.

Section 6

Reinstall in reverse order

Platform-specific
  1. Reinstall the GPU cage. Tighten the screws in the order shown in Figure 4 to [x N·m].
  2. Reconnect cables [C1–C4]. Check each connector is fully seated.
  3. Reinstall the air baffle and cover.
  4. Reinstall the server in the rack and reconnect the rear cables to their labels.
✔Checkpoint: count the removed parts against the parts reinstalled. Nothing should be left on the mat.
Why it's written this way

Service manuals copied between repairs sometimes remove seven parts and reinstall five. A parts count catches that on the floor, whatever the source document says.

Section 7

Test and return to service

Reusable module
  1. Power on. Confirm there are no fault LEDs.
  2. Run [post-repair diagnostic], and confirm the memory count and the DIMM in slot D7 pass.
  3. Update the ticket with the old and new serial numbers, then close it.
  4. Release the node back to production.
Why it's written this way

A repair isn't done until the node is proven healthy. Recording serials links this ticket to the RMA and to repeat-offender tracking if the same slot fails again.

What this shows

Modular sections

Sections 1, 2, 3 and 7 are reused across every repair on the platform. Sections 4 to 6 are the new writing, which keeps the yearly compliance load down.

Safety hierarchy

WARNING marks a risk of injury, CAUTION a risk of equipment damage, and NOTE handling or process tips: staff safety first, then outage risk, then component damage.

Written for failure

The parts count, keyed-notch note and cable labels exist because of the mistakes a tired technician on a night shift is most likely to make.

Compliance in the header

The manual revision, procedure version and review date are what the yearly compliance check verifies.
