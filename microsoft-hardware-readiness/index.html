Overview

Seven stages, five feedback loops

Stages HR-1 to HR-6 belong to the readiness pillar. Once a platform reaches growth, the sustaining pillar takes over HR-7. The loops below the track show where findings flow back upstream.

  1. L1Lab serviceability bugs go back to hardware engineering and the manufacturer for design fixes.
  2. L2Service manual defects go back to the SI, OEM or ODM for a corrected revision.
  3. L3Pilot-site feedback and new manual revisions reopen procedures before growth.
  4. L4Field feedback, escalations and incident patterns become procedure changes.
  5. L5Retrofits, fix-on-fail, diagnostic fixes and ticketing feature requests go back to engineering and suppliers.

Operating rules

Rules that apply across stages

Risk priority

  1. Staff safety
  2. Outage risk
  3. Component damage

Order used when deciding what a procedure must guard against. HM

NUDD prioritization

New, unique, difficult or different from what DC operations already handle gets platform-specific content first. GPU platforms almost always qualify, then compute, storage and network. HM

Write for the worst case

Procedures assume the least experienced technician on a long night shift. Ask what happens if a step is skipped, reversed or done with the wrong part. HM

Extreme ownership

An issue outside your scope still gets carried to the team that can fix it and followed to resolution. HM
