C1

A year of backlog to get to green

The seat has effectively been empty for about a year. GPU work has kept moving, but compute and storage platforms have a backlog. The first goal is to get every platform to green. HM

How I would handle it

  • In the first two weeks, list every platform with its procedure status, its pilot and growth dates, and its ticket volume.
  • Rank the list by NUDD first, then by how soon each platform reaches growth, then by repair volume.
  • Agree with my manager on what "green" means per platform, so progress is measured the same way every week.
  • Split the queue between me, the two vendor tech writers and the service engineers, and share a weekly burn-down.
Delta DR build recoveryDelta Air Lines, 2018
Situation
A contractor-led, 120-rack converged-infrastructure DR build had stalled after about a year.
Action
I took it over mid-stream and owned the remaining half of the hardware delivery. I restructured the sequencing across supply chain, the hardware and cabling vendors, the facility team and engineering, and reported progress against a recovery baseline.
Result
Delivered against the executive recovery baseline in August 2018.

Opening lineI have taken over stalled work before. The first thing I do is make the backlog visible and agree on what done looks like, then re-sequence around the highest-risk items.

C2

Ramping up on a new fleet while it gets busy

An external hire has a lot to absorb, and new projects are about to start. The role is leveled at TPM II to allow ramp-up time. HM The JD asks for leading virtual teams to a plan of record and influencing without direct control. JD

How I would handle it

  • Map who owns what in the first month: CHIE, the readiness team, learning and development, sustaining, the blue and red teams, and the ticketing engineering team.
  • Join lab buildup and teardown sessions from the start, since that is where the content begins.
  • Read through the existing modular procedure library and sit in on escalations to learn how technicians use it.
  • Set 30, 60 and 90 day checkpoints with my manager on what I own by each one.
ByteDance planning and delivery frameworkByteDance, 2025 to present
Situation
When I joined, the global edge team had been through several reorganizations, and it was unclear which team owned what when issues came up.
Action
With my manager's support, I brought the functional leads into a cross-functional virtual team. We mapped the process, assigned owners across engineering, supply chain, operations and finance, wrote the SOPs and workflow documents, and defined cross-team SLAs.
Result
The APAC, EMEA and NA/LATAM teams adopted it, and it is now standard practice.

Opening lineWhen I start somewhere new, I learn the system by finding out who owns each step, and I put that into a plan everyone agrees on.

C3

GPU, liquid cooling and new safety work

GPU platforms are almost always new, unique, difficult or different. Liquid-cooling processes such as coolant sampling and lockout/tagout are still being worked out, and IT technicians are now doing work that critical environments staff used to own. HM

How I would handle it

  • Be clear about my depth: GPU knowledge from structured self-study and a home lab, server platform depth from NPI work.
  • Pair with the service engineer who has been writing the GPU content, and with CHIE in the lab.
  • Have the critical environments team review every lockout/tagout and coolant step before it ships.
  • Treat EHS content as required at pilot, never deferred to a later revision. Safety comes first, then outage risk, then component damage.
Server platform NPI and a home GPU labByteDance, 2025 to present; personal
Situation
ByteDance needed next-generation cache and compute platforms, with six candidate configurations for each server type.
Action
I ran the evaluation with hardware engineering, supply chain and component vendors, weighing end-user video start latency against cost per CPU core. Separately, I built a home lab server with RTX 4090 GPUs. Seating the CPU correctly and applying thermal paste took several checks, which showed me how easily a repair goes wrong.
Result
Each server type narrowed to one standard configuration. My CDCP certification also covers electrical distribution and mechanical cooling concepts.

Gap to name honestly: I have not written procedures for liquid-cooled GPU racks. My answer is how I learn fast and who I would lean on, not a claim of direct experience.

Opening lineGPU and liquid cooling are where I would learn most. I would get into the lab early and pair with the people who know it, and I would not let safety content slip past pilot.

C4

Manual revisions, configuration drift and compliance load

New service manual revisions can send procedures back for rework between pilot and growth. Supply changes create several configurations of one platform, and every published procedure gets a yearly compliance review. HM

How I would handle it

  • Record which manual revision each procedure and section is based on, so a new revision shows exactly what is affected.
  • Use modular sections, so a revision changes one section rather than every procedure that uses it.
  • Decide reuse or rewrite per hardware generation with CHIE, instead of assuming two similar platforms match.
  • Write only the content that is needed, because each extra procedure adds compliance work every year.
Windows Server and SQL Server migrationPaylocity, 2022 to 2025
Situation
I owned the upgrade and migration of about 6,000 Windows Server VMs and 300 SQL Server instances across dev, QA, pre-prod and production.
Action
Midway through, I gathered structured feedback from the teams doing the work and changed the workflow where it was causing repeat effort.
Result
Rework dropped, and we limited the load on team availability and the impact on production.

Opening lineContent that keeps changing needs a clear record of what it is based on, so a change upstream shows you exactly what to update downstream.

C5

Happy-path procedures and field-only problems

Procedures that work in the lab can fail on the floor, and reviewers without operations experience often miss it. Some problems only appear in production: exothermic events, customer-induced damage, unstable rails. HM

How I would handle it

  • Review every procedure against the failure cases: a part reversed, a step skipped or out of order, the wrong part fitted.
  • Write for the least experienced technician on a long night shift.
  • Line up one or two technicians at each pilot site for direct feedback before growth.
  • Go through procedure feedback and escalations with the blue team each month and turn repeat patterns into procedure controls.
Diagnostic and post-repair test proceduresByteDance, 2025 to present
Situation
Each new server platform needed operational content before sites could support it.
Action
With hardware engineering, DC operations and OEM/ODM partners, I developed the diagnostic procedures, the post-repair test procedures that return repaired servers to production, and the asset records.
Result
The content rolled out to sites across APAC, EMEA and NA/LATAM and is now standard for new platforms.

Second story if asked about incidents: at Paylocity I rebuilt the reliability review around incident trends, post-mortems and RCA accountability, which contributed to about a 40% drop in Sev 1 and Sev 2 incidents within a year. At Delta, the operations knowledge base and four week-long training sessions I ran significantly reduced escalations from Level 2 to Level 3.

Opening lineI write procedures for what goes wrong, not just for what should happen, and I use field feedback to close the gaps the lab missed.

C6

Limited control over suppliers

Microsoft largely buys what OEMs sell, has some input with ODMs, and depends on suppliers to fix service manuals and hardware. Fixes go through the readiness team and CHIE. HM The JD calls for developing procedures with OEM and ODM partners. JD

How I would handle it

  • Report each issue with evidence: the step reference, photos from the lab, and the impact on safety, outage risk or component damage.
  • Push for a manufacturing fix first, and add a procedure control only when the hardware can't change.
  • Keep a tracked list of open supplier issues and review it with the readiness team and CHIE until each closes.
  • Use waiver conditions to make sure a pilot-cluster fix carries into follow-up clusters.
OEM and ODM breakfix and lifecycleByteDance, 2025 to present
Situation
Our fleet runs Dell and ODM platforms from Inventec, Quanta and Foxconn, plus Arista switching.
Action
I manage supply and deployment from BOM definition and forecast-to-PO through staging, burn-in and DOA/RMA handling with VARs. I track hardware failure and RMA trends by server type.
Result
I use those trends to set decommissioning schedules that protect fleet availability and keep capacity in service.

Opening lineWith suppliers I bring data, not complaints. A clear defect with its impact attached gets fixed faster than a general request.

C7

Getting ticketing changes prioritized

When the ticketing system can't route the right procedure, the fix needs a business requirements document with data and business justification, then a place on the engineering team's priority list. A real link to GPU capacity restoration raises priority. HM

How I would handle it

  • Measure the impact from ticket data: misrouted tickets, extra technician time, and delay in returning capacity.
  • Tie the request to GPU capacity only where the data shows the link.
  • Propose a design, and stay open to a better one from the engineering team.
  • Follow the request through their prioritization until it ships, then confirm routing works on real tickets.
Server reuse programByteDance, 2025 to present
Situation
Decommissioned central datacenter servers could serve edge sites, but reuse had to be justified against buying new.
Action
I qualified them through cost-benefit analysis and performance testing, and used the results to make the case per server type.
Result
Cost reduction ranged from 20% to 50% per server type compared with new procurement.

Second story if asked about tooling: at Delta I negotiated a Cisco bare-metal provisioning package and built automation on it. Per-server provisioning dropped from 20 hours to 4, and the platform came in 2 months ahead of schedule.

Opening lineA change request competes with every other request, so I make the business case with numbers the other team can check.

C8

A stretched team, a shared space and a remote manager

Service engineers are covering procedures on top of escalations. One of them wants to keep working on GPU content, and the new TPM should keep that person involved. The manager is in Dublin, and the role sits in Redmond. HM

How I would handle it

  • Take the procedure writing load off the service engineers first, so they can get back to escalations.
  • Agree a defined piece of GPU content with the engineer who wants to stay involved, inside one shared plan that I own.
  • Credit that work visibly in reviews and updates.
  • Send my manager a short written update each week before the end of his day, with a RAID list he can review on his own time.
