The platform

What a GB200 NVL72 rack contains

72Blackwell GPUs
36Grace CPUs
18compute trays, 4 GPUs and 2 CPUs each
9NVLink switch trays
  • NVLink spine of copper cable cartridges at the rear joins all 72 GPUs
  • Power shelves feed a busbar instead of individual cords to PDUs
  • Cold plates and manifolds with quick-disconnect fittings, cooled by an HRU or CDU
  • CPU memory is soldered, so there is no DIMM swap on compute trays
Why it's NUDD

New, unique, difficult and different

  • New: rack-scale design where the whole rack behaves as one system
  • Unique: liquid cooling and busbar power, unlike the air-cooled fleet
  • Difficult: heavy trays, coolant handling, dense NVLink connections
  • Different: lockout/tagout moves IT technicians into work critical environments used to own

So it goes to the top of the priority list, and nearly every repair needs a platform-specific procedure.

HR-7 measures

What to track once GB200 is in production

  • Procedure coverage at pilot and at growth
  • Tickets by repair type, and the share routed to a platform-specific procedure
  • Repeat-offender tickets on the same tray
  • Time to restore GPU capacity after a fault
  • Coolant leaks and other EHS events
  • Procedure feedback received, accepted and declined
  • Tray failure rate and RMA returns
For the interview

Questions to raise with the panel

  1. For GB-class racks, which repairs happen on site, and which trays go back to the ODM?
  2. Who owns lockout/tagout on HRU work today: site services, critical environments, or both?
  3. How is authoring split between the TPM, the tech writers and the GPU service engineer?
  4. How much of the GB200 content carried over to GB300, and how was that decided?
  5. Which readiness gates most often hold up a GPU pilot?
